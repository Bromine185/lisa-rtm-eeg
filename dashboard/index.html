The CDA: the brain signal this dataset is about

Contralateral minus ipsilateral voltage over posterior scalp, averaged over all analysed people, for 2, 4 and 6 remembered squares. The shaded window (300–900 ms) is where its amplitude is measured. Bigger (more negative) means more held in memory.

Labs differ in attention, and that is their capacity

Each dot is a lab. Attention = accuracy-based share of 2-item trials actually encoded; capacity K = Cowan's K at 4 and 6 items. Lab means of analysed people.

Capacity K of every person

Eye drift toward the remembered side

Horizontal eye signal (µV, toward the cued side) during the delay, by set size. It follows the CDA's shape: up from 2 to 4, flat to 6.

CDA waveform by set size

Mean ± standard error over the lab's analysed people, contralateral minus ipsilateral, µV.

A single trial's CDA is mostly noise

Distribution of single-trial CDA amplitudes (µV, 300–900 ms, clean trials). The averaged effect is under 1 µV; single trials spread over tens.

How much of one trial the average predicts

Coherent fraction by frequency: the share of a single trial's lateralized energy that the leave-one-out average of its condition captures. Almost all of it sits below 4 Hz.

1/f exponent against capacity

Individual alpha frequency

Forecasting capacity in an unseen lab

CRPS skill over a forecast that knows nothing (left out one lab at a time). "Centred in lab" removes lab differences; "lab only" knows just the lab's mean.

Predicting a missing electrode

Median coherent fraction (share predicted) by frequency band for three interpolators. Low frequencies are almost fully predictable; above 30 Hz about half.

…and the level each one outputs

Output energy / true energy. The best averaging map (Wiener) goes quiet by exactly its coherent fraction; one Gaussian draw keeps the level at 1.
